# Brainstorm: Architectures for All-Horizon Stable Deterministic Rollout

**Date**: 2026-04-24  •  **Status**: research-mode brainstorm, no implementation yet
**Context**: rescor_mamba_rand wins H=15 by ~15× over rens K=32 but **catastrophically diverges at H=100** (cos_div ≈ 0.50 = random). rens is mediocre everywhere but does not blow up. We want both.

This document is a planning artifact only. Posterior-style / stochastic-latent fixes are explicitly out of scope (user-excluded). All ideas below stay deterministic.

---

## 1. Failure-mode analysis: why does mamba diverge at H=100?

**Empirical signature.** mamba_rand cuts step-1 MSE by 11× and H=15 abs MSE by 15× vs rens, but `(H=100 abs MSE) / (step-1 MSE)` is ~2 × 10^5 — orders of magnitude worse than rens's 1.7 × 10^3 ratio over the same horizon. Cos_div at H=100 is 0.50, i.e. the prediction has rotated to a random direction in latent space. In contrast rens degrades smoothly (47× at H=15, plateau 45× at H=50/100) without ever leaving a bounded latent neighborhood (§60 plateau pattern). So the comparison is **not** "mamba is worse at long horizons in a smooth way" — it is "mamba has an additional regime change between H=50 and H=100 that rens does not".

**Hypothesized mechanisms** (most-likely first):

(H1) **K=4 frame buffer becomes a closed-loop error amplifier.** mamba's input at step t is `[x_{t-3}, x_{t-2}, x_{t-1}, x_t]`. Once step 1 is generated by the model, every subsequent buffer fill is *self-prediction*. At H=4 the buffer is 100% model output. The Mamba SSM was trained on buffers of *real* frames; the manifold of model-output buffers is slightly off-distribution, and the SSM amplifies this off-distribution component because its selective dt/B/C parameters were tuned to extract trajectory velocity/acceleration *of real data*. Around H=50–100 the off-distribution component crosses some implicit linearisation radius and the trajectory leaves the neighborhood. Rens has no buffer (single-frame input), so no such closed loop exists.

(H2) **Mamba's residual learns to over-correct on training distribution.** Step-1 win is so large (11×) that almost certainly the residual head has high effective capacity relative to the rens-mean baseline — it's not a "tiny correction." In autoregression, a residual that's accurate on real frames and large in magnitude is exactly the residual that does the most damage when input is slightly off-manifold: the correction is computed in a regime where it was never trained, but it is still applied at full magnitude. Rens's 321-param NCA is small enough that it doesn't have this problem.

(H3) **No contraction / fixed-point structure.** rens's CML-mean is bounded ([1e-4, 1-1e-4]) and locally contractive in many directions because the K=32 r-ensemble averages out divergent components. Mamba adds a learned linear+SSM term whose Jacobian magnitude is unconstrained. If the per-step Jacobian's spectral radius >1 along even one direction, error grows exponentially with H — which is exactly the H=100 cos_div=0.50 signature.

(H4) **No self-correcting drive.** rens uses the input itself as `drive` inside the CML loop (`(1-β)·physics + β·drive`), so each frozen reservoir always pulls the state back toward the current input. mamba's temporal feature has no such anchor — once it drifts, nothing reels it back.

These four are not mutually exclusive; (H1)+(H3) together is the most-parsimonious story. Most architectural ideas below target one or more of them.

## 2. Architectural ideas

12 ideas, ordered roughly cheap→ambitious. Each has: forward sketch (with shapes), horizon-by-horizon honest assessment, params, effort, compute, risk.

Notation throughout: `B`=batch, `C`=in_channels (typically 1, or 2 for KEEP-posterior latent stream), `H,W`=spatial (16×16), `K_rens`=32 reservoirs, `K_t`=temporal context (currently 4), `d`=mamba d_model.

### Idea 1: **Anchored-Mamba** — convex-combine mamba output with a contractive anchor
**Summary.** Apply mamba normally, but every step blend the output with a known-contractive baseline whose blend weight is *fixed* (not learned at low value, hard-coded).

**Forward.**
```
x_now    = x_seq[:, -1]                          # (B, C, H, W)
cml_mean = rens(x_now)                            # frozen, contractive baseline
mamba_corr = mamba_residual(x_seq)                # (B, C, H, W) — current rescor_mamba output minus cml_mean
α = sigmoid(scalar_param)                         # 1 trained scalar, init s.t. α≈0.7
out = cml_mean + α * mamba_corr                   # bound mamba's influence
```
At rollout time we *additionally* schedule α_eff(t) = α · γ^t (γ ≈ 0.99) so mamba's contribution decays with horizon. γ is a *fixed* deployment knob (not trained), not a stochastic injection — purely deterministic horizon-dependent attenuation.

**Why short:** at small t, α_eff ≈ α ≈ 0.7, mamba contributes most of its win.
**Why mid:** at H=15, α_eff ≈ 0.86 of α — modest attenuation, still close to mamba.
**Why long:** at H=100, α_eff ≈ 0.37 of α, model approaches pure rens which has bounded plateau (§60). H=100 catastrophe replaced by H=100 plateau — same as rens, no worse.
**Tradeoff:** fundamentally ceilings mamba's potential at long horizon. We accept this.

**Params:** +1 (α scalar) — negligible.
**Effort:** ~1 day. Modify `ResCorMamba.forward`, add α scheduling utility.
**Compute:** identical to mamba (CPU-feasible, currently ~5K trained params).
**Risk:** the α schedule is hand-tuned; might need γ swept. Won't recover mamba's full win even at H=15 (the 0.7 cap costs ~30% of the residual). Step-1 win likely shrinks to ~5× over rens instead of 11×.

### Idea 2: **Drift-Penalty Training** — auxiliary loss on multi-step Jacobian norm
**Summary.** Keep the architecture exactly as `ResCorMamba`. Add a training loss term that explicitly penalizes the per-step Jacobian's spectral radius along the prediction direction (target H3).

**Forward.** Identical to existing ResCorMamba.
**Loss.** `total = mse(x̂_{t+1}, x_{t+1}) + λ_drift · ||∂x̂_{t+1}/∂x_t||_op` estimated via a 1-step finite-difference probe (cheap: `x_{t+1}^a = f([x_seq, x_t]); x_{t+1}^b = f([x_seq, x_t + ε·u]); penalty = ||x_{t+1}^b - x_{t+1}^a|| / ε`). With u a random unit vector resampled each batch.

**Why short:** training-time only; no inference cost. step-1 unchanged.
**Why mid:** if model learns an effectively contractive map (op norm <1), error decays exponentially across H=15.
**Why long:** if op norm ≤1 the H=100 catastrophe is impossible by construction.
**Tradeoff:** fights with the MSE objective. At λ_drift too low → no effect; too high → model collapses to identity. There exists a sweet spot or there doesn't.

**Params:** 0 added.
**Effort:** ~2 days (loss term + training-loop plumbing + one λ sweep).
**Compute:** training ~2× slower (one extra forward per batch). Inference unchanged.
**Risk:** Mikhaeil et al. 2022 (already cited in the codebase) warns gradients through chaotic dynamics are noisy; this loss probes exactly that gradient. Could increase variance enough to lose the H=15 mamba advantage.

### Idea 3: **Mamba-Skip-K** — temporal context only every k steps during rollout
**Summary.** mamba sees every step at training time but only every k=3 frames at inference. The other 2 frames are predicted with mamba **frozen** (its contribution recomputed from the same buffer slot).

**Forward (rollout).**
```
for t in 0..H:
    if t % k == 0:
        buffer.append(x_t); buffer.pop_front()
        cached_temporal = mamba_feat(buffer)
    out = cml_mean(x_t) + nca([x_t, cml_mean, cached_temporal])
```
The buffer freezes for k-1 steps, so the SSM does not run over an increasingly self-polluted buffer (target H1). Periodic refresh keeps temporal info fresh enough to help short horizons.

**Why short:** at H=4 with k=3, buffer has been refreshed once — temporal feat is fresh, mamba helps.
**Why mid:** H=15 with k=3 → 5 refreshes — modest accumulation of self-prediction in buffer but limited.
**Why long:** H=100 with k=3 → 33 refreshes — still polluted, but evolution of pollution is slowed by 3×, the H=100 catastrophe horizon shifts to H=300.
**Tradeoff:** doesn't fix divergence, just delays it. Buys a constant factor.

**Params:** 0 added.
**Effort:** ~1 day (modify rollout driver only; train protocol unchanged).
**Compute:** rollout ~3× cheaper (mamba runs k×fewer times); training unchanged.
**Risk:** simple delay tactic — still ultimately diverges. Frozen-temporal during inference but mamba was trained to expect fresh temporal each step → train-test gap. Could lose the mamba H=15 win entirely if k chosen poorly.

### Idea 4: **Gated-Hybrid (rens ⊕ mamba)** — learned per-cell gate over short and long-term predictors
**Summary.** Run rens and mamba in parallel. Per-cell learned gate decides which to trust. Gate is conditioned on a *drift signal* — a measure of how far we are from training distribution.

**Forward.**
```
cml_mean = rens(x_now)                           # (B, C, H, W) — long-term-stable
mamba_pred = mamba_residual(x_seq) + cml_mean    # (B, C, H, W) — short-term-accurate
drift_signal = ||x_now - cml_mean||_per_cell      # (B, 1, H, W) — proxy for H1
gate = σ(Conv2d([x_now, cml_mean, drift_signal])) # (B, 1, H, W)
out = gate * mamba_pred + (1-gate) * cml_mean
```
At training, gate is unconstrained. At inference, drift_signal grows during free-run rollout (model output diverges from rens-mean), which closes the gate toward rens. This is *implicitly* horizon-dependent without a hand-coded schedule.

**Why short:** drift_signal small at H=1, gate ≈ 1, full mamba.
**Why mid:** drift_signal moderate, gate partially open.
**Why long:** drift_signal large, gate closed → behaves as rens (bounded plateau).

**Params:** +`Conv2d(C+C+1, hidden=8, 3) + Conv2d(8, 1, 1)` ≈ 100–200 params.
**Effort:** ~2 days.
**Compute:** runs both rens (cheap, frozen) and mamba (already running). +5%.
**Risk:** training the gate is delicate — `drift_signal` ≈ 0 throughout training (teacher-forced), so the gate-on-drift axis is undertrained at distribution shift time. Mitigation: add training-time noise to x_now ONLY for the gate input, not for the predictors. (Note: noise injection on the *predictor* path is excluded by §61's negative result; this is on the gate path only, which is a different beast.)

### Idea 5: **Contractive-Mamba** — wrap mamba's residual in a hard Lipschitz cap
**Summary.** Direct attack on H3. Constrain the mamba residual's per-step magnitude to be a strict-contractive multiple of the *baseline contraction* of rens.

**Forward.**
```
cml_mean = rens(x_now)
raw_corr = mamba_residual(x_seq)                  # may be unbounded
norm = ||raw_corr||_per_cell                      # (B, 1, H, W)
ρ = ||x_now - cml_mean||_per_cell                 # rens's natural step magnitude
clipped_corr = raw_corr * min(1, λ * ρ / (norm + 1e-6))  # Lipschitz cap
out = cml_mean + clipped_corr
```
λ < 1 enforces strict contraction (mamba's correction is at most λ× the rens baseline magnitude). λ=0.5 means mamba can never push the prediction further from cml_mean than rens does naturally.

**Why short:** at H=1, rens's ρ is large (it's not a great single-step predictor) → cap is loose → mamba's correction gets through.
**Why mid:** ρ stays comparable to its train-time scale → loose cap → mamba helps.
**Why long:** in the catastrophe regime, raw_corr's norm explodes but ρ doesn't (rens stays bounded) → cap kicks in hard → mamba contribution attenuates exactly when it would have caused divergence.

**Params:** +1 (λ scalar) or 0 (fix λ=0.5). Effectively 0.
**Effort:** ~2 days.
**Compute:** +1 pointwise op per cell. Negligible.
**Risk:** the cap fires only when raw_corr is *already* large, so at small drifts it does nothing. If the failure mode is multi-step accumulation of *small* corrections (H1 not H3), this idea misses. Co-applied with Idea 4 might cover both.

### Idea 6: **Multi-Resolution Temporal** — pyramid of temporal contexts at different strides
**Summary.** Three Mamba blocks, each over a different temporal stride (Δt=1, Δt=4, Δt=16). Long-stride mamba sees a much longer effective horizon with the same buffer length, providing low-frequency stability info.

**Forward.**
```
x_seq: (B, K_t=16, C, H, W)
seq_fast = x_seq[:, -4:]                         # last 4 frames (Δt=1)
seq_med  = x_seq[:, -16::4]                       # 4 frames at Δt=4
seq_slow = x_seq[:, ::4][:, -4:]                  # 4 frames spanning all 16 steps (Δt=4 effective from front)
feat_fast = mamba_fast(seq_fast)
feat_med  = mamba_med(seq_med)
feat_slow = mamba_slow(seq_slow)
nca_in = [x_now, cml_mean, feat_fast, feat_med, feat_slow]
out = cml_mean + nca(nca_in)
```

**Why short:** fast scale carries velocity info, full short-horizon win retained.
**Why mid:** medium scale catches drift before it accumulates.
**Why long:** slow scale provides a "low-pass" memory that resists the H1 buffer-pollution dynamic — at Δt=4 the buffer at H=100 is filled with predictions spanning a much wider context, so any single corrupted frame is averaged out by the SSM.

**Params:** 3× current mamba ≈ 12–16K trained params.
**Effort:** ~3–4 days. Requires K_t=16 buffer, which means longer training data sequences.
**Compute:** 3× mamba forward. Still CPU-feasible.
**Risk:** still has the H1 closed-loop issue, just at three different rates. Could just delay the catastrophe to a later H, similar to Idea 3 but with more parameters. Multi-resolution is worth trying because each scale's noise floor is different.

### Idea 7: **Recurrent Hidden State Across Rollout** — explicit `h_t` carried between inference steps
**Summary.** Currently, mamba is re-run from scratch on the K_t buffer at every rollout step. Instead, expose mamba's internal SSM state `h_t ∈ R^{d_inner × d_state}` and carry it across rollout steps as a true recurrent state. The buffer becomes K_t=1 (just the latest frame).

**Forward.**
```
# at rollout step t
seq = in_proj(x_now)                                       # (B*H*W, 1, d)
h_t, mamba_out = mamba_step(seq, h_{t-1})                  # h_t: (B*H*W, d_inner, d_state)
temporal_feat = reshape(mamba_out, (B, d, H, W))
out = cml_mean + nca([x_now, cml_mean, temporal_feat])
# carry h_t to next step
```
At training, train on full sequences with backprop-through-time over H_train steps (e.g. H_train=10), so the model learns to use its hidden state stably.

**Why short:** the hidden state from a few real frames is well-conditioned — short horizon prediction quality preserved.
**Why mid:** as long as H_train is matched to mid horizon, the network is *trained* in a regime where hidden state stability matters → directly attacks H1.
**Why long:** if backprop window is long enough, gradient signal propagates from H_train to teach a contractive recurrence. Mamba's discretization (`exp(dt·A)` with A < 0) is intrinsically dissipative — actually *more* stable than the K_t-buffer approach for long H.

**Params:** unchanged from current ResCorMamba (~5.3K). The hidden state is data, not parameters.
**Effort:** 4–6 days. Substantial pipeline work: training loop over multi-step rollouts, gradient checkpointing, careful handling of `h_0` initialization.
**Compute:** training: O(H_train × current step cost). Inference: cheaper than current (no buffer to re-process). H_train=10 → training ~10× slower, definitely needs GPU.
**Risk:** BPTT gradients through chaotic dynamics is the exact thing Mikhaeil 2022 warns about. Could be unstable to train. But this is the architecturally cleanest answer to H1.

### Idea 8: **Reservoir-Conditioned Attention** — attend over the K=32 reservoir bank instead of averaging
**Summary.** rens currently does uniform 1/K averaging over 32 reservoirs. Replace this with cross-attention from a learned query (computed from `x_seq` history) to the K=32 reservoir outputs treated as keys/values. The query, conditioned on temporal context, *picks which reservoir matches the current dynamics best*.

**Forward.**
```
res_stack = rens._run_batched(x_now)              # (B, 32, C, H, W) — frozen
res_kv    = flatten(res_stack)                    # (B, 32, C*H*W)
query     = mamba_summary(x_seq)                  # (B, d_q) — single global query per batch
# OR per-cell:
query_pc  = small_conv(temporal_feat)             # (B, d_q, H, W)
attn_w    = softmax(W_q query · W_k res_kv / sqrt(d))  # (B, 32) or (B, 32, H, W)
weighted  = (attn_w[:,:,None,...] * res_stack).sum(1)  # (B, C, H, W)
out       = weighted + nca([x_now, weighted, temporal_feat])
```

**Why short:** mamba's temporal context picks the most-relevant reservoir → likely better than 1/K average for any given current state. Plausibly recovers some of mamba's H=15 win without using mamba *as a residual*.
**Why mid:** the reservoir bank is frozen and bounded, so even with attention weighting the output is a convex combination of bounded vectors — no Jacobian explosion possible.
**Why long:** reservoir bank is the H=100 stability anchor. Attention over it picks a stable basis at every step.

**Params:** ~`32 + d_q × d_q + 32` for global; ~`small_conv + d_q × d_q` ≈ 1–4K for per-cell. Still has mamba as the query computation (~5K). Total ~5–10K.
**Effort:** 3 days.
**Compute:** trivial extra over current ResCorMamba (the K=32 forward dominates).
**Risk:** attention output is still a convex combination of reservoir outputs — capped at the bank's expressivity. If the H=15 win in mamba comes from learning *new* corrections beyond what the bank can represent, this idea ceilings short-horizon wins. Plausible mid-ground: combine with a small mamba residual gated by Idea 4 / 5.

### Idea 9: **Manifold-Projection Layer** — learn a low-dim manifold and project predictions onto it
**Summary.** Train a tiny autoencoder (CPU-cheap) on the training latents to learn the data manifold. At each rollout step, project the prediction onto the manifold before feeding it back as input. Projection bound = the AE's reconstruction error on training data.

**Forward.**
```
x̂_{t+1} = current_model(x_seq)                  # (B, C, H, W)
z = ae_encoder(x̂_{t+1})                          # (B, d_z) — d_z = 8 or 16
x̂_proj = ae_decoder(z)                           # (B, C, H, W) — on-manifold version
# during rollout, use x̂_proj as the next frame, not x̂
buffer.append(x̂_proj)
```
The AE's image set is the data manifold (approximately). Off-manifold drift is *erased* every step — directly attacks the H1 closed-loop pollution.

**Why short:** at H=1, x̂ is already close to manifold, AE projection noise ≈ AE reconstruction noise. Slight degradation at small H.
**Why mid:** drift starts to develop, AE projection eats it back. Stability ceiling = AE quality.
**Why long:** as long as AE generalizes, projection prevents drift forever. If AE doesn't generalize, predictions get pulled toward the AE's training-data centroid, which is benign (bounded).

**Params:** ~`Conv2d(C, 16, 3) + Conv2d(16, d_z, 16) + Conv2d(d_z, 16, 16) + Conv2d(16, C, 3)` ≈ 5–15K depending on d_z. Adds to existing model's parameters.
**Effort:** 3–4 days. Train AE on Crafter latents first (cheap, single epoch), then plug it in.
**Compute:** +1 forward pass through AE per inference step. Trivial.
**Risk:** classical autoencoder failure modes — manifold mode collapse, blur. AE quality directly caps prediction quality. Requires AE generalization to *unseen* free-run trajectories, which is exactly what we don't have evidence for. Open question: should AE be trained on real frames only, or also on rollout outputs?

### Idea 10: **Iterated-Refinement Stack** — multiple mamba+rens blocks with intermediate clamping
**Summary.** Stack L blocks `[rens → mamba → clamp]^L`. Each block is an *iterated refinement* of the prediction; the clamp at the end of each block prevents intermediate drift. Conceptually: take a Newton-step toward the prediction L times, each time anchored by rens.

**Forward.**
```
state = x_now
for l in range(L):
    mean_l = rens_l(state)                           # frozen, contractive
    mamba_l = mamba_residual_l([buffer, state])      # learned correction
    state = clamp(mean_l + mamba_l, 0, 1)            # clamp prevents per-block drift
out = state
```
The L iterations all happen *inside one inference step*. The buffer for the temporal feature is the input buffer (not iterated).

**Why short:** L iterations refine — small mamba residuals compound *constructively* if trained well.
**Why mid:** intermediate clamping bounds each refinement, so no single iteration can over-correct.
**Why long:** L=2 or 3 gives more representational depth without adding the K_t-buffer pollution risk; rollout-time, only the final state goes back into the buffer, so per-step pollution is no worse than current mamba.

**Params:** L × current mamba ≈ 10–15K for L=2.
**Effort:** 3 days. Modeled as `ResCorRensDeep` already exists in the codebase (rens stages, no mamba); extend it to interleave mamba.
**Compute:** L× current. Borderline CPU.
**Risk:** doesn't fundamentally fix the H1 buffer-pollution dynamic. If mamba's per-step contribution is unstable, L iterations of it within one step is L× worse, not better.

### Idea 11: **Predictive Coding / Energy Refinement** — iterative correction by energy descent
**Summary.** Define a learned energy `E(x̂; x_seq) = ||x̂ - mamba(x̂, x_seq)||² + λ ||x̂ - rens(x_now)||²`. At inference, perform K steps of gradient descent on E w.r.t. x̂, starting from `x̂_0 = rens(x_now)`. The fixed point is by construction a x̂ that *agrees with* both rens and mamba simultaneously. (Distinct from Idea 10: this is iteration to a fixed point of a learned energy, not a forward-stack.)

**Forward.**
```
x̂ = rens(x_now)                                    # initial guess
for k in range(K_iters):
    ∇E = ∂E/∂x̂                                     # via PyTorch autograd
    x̂ = x̂ - η · ∇E                                  # ~0.1, K=5
out = x̂
```

**Why short:** finds a minimum of E; if E is well-shaped, the minimum is the right answer.
**Why mid:** the rens regularizer term keeps x̂ close to a contractive baseline → bounded drift.
**Why long:** the iteration *inside* the step is dissipative (each gradient step shrinks E) — net rollout dynamics inherit this dissipativity if K_iters and η are right.

**Params:** unchanged from current. Possibly +1 (λ scalar) or +1 (η scalar).
**Effort:** 4–5 days. Requires careful inference-time autograd (or hand-coded gradient if forms are simple). Nontrivial but tractable.
**Compute:** K_iters × current. K=5 → 5× slower. May need GPU for serious experimentation.
**Risk:** energy-minimisation as an inference primitive is finicky — η too high → divergence inside the step; η too low → no progress. Convergence-depth-as-hyperparameter problem. Might be elegant but unstable.

### Idea 12: **Frozen-Backbone, Trained Tail** — separate the sequence model into "stable trunk" + "expressive head" with disjoint update rules
**Summary.** Two-part architecture: (a) a *frozen* contractive trunk = the existing rens K=32; (b) an expressive *trained* head that operates on the trunk's output but has its weights *spectrally normalized* (SN). SN guarantees the head's Lipschitz constant is bounded by 1, so the composition is contractive by construction.

**Forward.**
```
trunk_out = rens(x_now)                              # contractive, frozen
head_in = [x_now, trunk_out, mamba_temporal_feat]    # mamba can be SN'd too
correction = SN_Conv2d(head_in, ..., out=C)          # spectrally-normed head
out = trunk_out + correction
```
Each Conv2d in the head is wrapped in `torch.nn.utils.spectral_norm`. SN is essentially free at inference (the largest singular value is precomputed) and keeps the head's per-step Jacobian bounded.

**Why short:** SN-bounded head is still expressive enough to learn step-1 corrections (this is well-established in stable GAN literature). Step-1 win likely 50–80% of mamba's full win.
**Why mid:** Lipschitz ≤ 1 guarantees error doesn't grow worse than linearly in H.
**Why long:** SN on every layer of the head + contractive trunk = provably contractive composition. H=100 catastrophe is mathematically impossible.

**Params:** unchanged. SN adds a power-iteration overhead per training step but no learnable params.
**Effort:** 2–3 days.
**Compute:** training slightly slower (power iteration); inference unchanged.
**Risk:** Lipschitz ≤ 1 is restrictive — empirically GANs use SN with relaxed bounds. Step-1 win may shrink more than expected (the paper-best mamba residuals may have local Lipschitz > 1 but only along *task-relevant* directions; SN cuts those equally). Worth trying as a "safe" lower-bound architecture.

## 3. Top-3 picks for the 1-week sprint

These are ordered by information-value-per-day, with explicit cheap/medium/ambitious coverage.

**Pick 1 (cheap, ~2 days): Idea 4 — Gated-Hybrid (rens ⊕ mamba) with drift signal.** Highest-EV cheap experiment. The drift signal is a directly testable hypothesis (does H1's buffer-pollution dynamic actually manifest as a measurable `||x̂ - cml_mean||` increase during rollout?), and the gate gives us *automatic* horizon-dependent attenuation without hand-coded scheduling. If the drift signal works as a free-run-degradation detector, that signal alone is reusable across all later ideas (5, 7, 11). Even on a negative result (gate stays open), we learn that drift-norm-on-rens-mean is not the right failure indicator, narrowing future search.

**Pick 2 (medium, ~3 days): Idea 12 — Spectral-Normalized Tail.** This is the *theoretically-cleanest* answer to H3. SN-on-every-conv has 30 years of stability theory behind it; if it costs us 30–40% of the H=15 win and eliminates the H=100 catastrophe entirely, we've found the deterministic stability lever. Cheap to implement (PyTorch ships `torch.nn.utils.spectral_norm`), low risk of total failure. Pairs naturally with Pick 1 (gate + SN'd predictor = belt + suspenders) if both work.

**Pick 3 (ambitious, ~6 days): Idea 7 — Recurrent Hidden State + BPTT.** This is the highest-ceiling architectural answer. If H1 (closed-loop buffer pollution) is the dominant failure mechanism — which the cos_div=0.50-only-at-H=100 pattern strongly suggests — then *removing the buffer entirely and replacing it with a true SSM hidden state trained with BPTT* is exactly the right structural change. Mamba's exp(dt·A) recurrence is intrinsically dissipative; the failure right now is that we're not letting it run as a recurrence (we re-process a buffer instead). This is the only idea that, if it works, plausibly preserves *all* of mamba's H=15 win while converging stably at H=100. High effort because BPTT through chaotic dynamics is the canonical hard problem of this codebase, but if it works it's the headline result.

## 4. Open questions / unknowns (need user input before any implementation)

1. **Param budget hard ceiling.** The brief says 5–50K. Does Idea 7 (still ~5K) or Idea 9 (5–15K) win priority over Idea 6 (12–16K) if all three are competitive? In particular, *is the AE in Idea 9 inside or outside the param budget* — it's a separate model trained separately, more like a frozen module.
